Export Signals
Four independent research findings. Each describes a storage condition that, historically, precedes a drawdown event on the next SAR pass. Baseline probability of an export drawdown on any given observation: 15%. When a signal is ACTIVE, the observed probability rises — shown per signal below. When multiple signals are active simultaneously, the composite probability is shown at top.
Composite P(drawdown)
— export probability
Composite rises when signals stack. Strongest combination shown. Baseline is 15% with no active signals.
How to read: Each card shows a market condition and the historical probability of an export drawdown when that condition is true. ACTIVE means the condition is met right now. It does not guarantee an export — it means odds are elevated based on observed history.

Export Coverage Ratio --
outflow ÷ inflow · per SAR pass
Export Coverage Ratio — how much oil left the terminal vs how much arrived from upstream fields on the same pass.

ECR > 1.2 (green) — exports exceed upstream inflow; terminal is drawing down reserves.

ECR ≈ 1 (grey, 0.85–1.2) — upstream inflow and exports are roughly equal; oil flows straight through.

ECR < 0.85 (red) — upstream inflow significantly exceeds exports; oil is accumulating in storage.
Days to Empty --
at current export rate · how long until storage is drained
How many days at the current export pace until the terminal would be fully drained.

< 40d — exports are fast relative to storage; active loading, high throughput.
> 90d — exports are slow relative to storage; oil is sitting, possible export slowdown.

Step 1 of 4

      
How a radar satellite measures oil

      

        Sentinel-1 sends radar pulses at the tanks. Each tank has a floating roof
        that rides on the oil surface — higher when full, lower when empty.
        The roof casts a shadow on the far tank wall; the satellite reads that shadow.
      

      

        

          

            

            
Full tank — roof is high, small shadow → low radar return

          

          

            

            
Empty tank — roof is low, large shadow → high radar return

          

          

            

            
Lower dB = more oil. OilRadar converts this to Mb across all 47 tanks.

          

        

      

    


    

      
Step 2 of 4

      
Data updates every ~6 days

      

        OilRadar processes a new Sentinel-1 SAR satellite pass over Kharg Island
        roughly every 6 days. Each pass refreshes all storage figures —
        you receive an email the moment new data is processed.
      

      

        

          
~6d

          

            
Revisit cycle

            
Sentinel-1 C-band SAR · descending orbit
47 floating-roof tanks · Kharg Island

            
You get an email on every new pass
