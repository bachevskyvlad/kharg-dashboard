How to read the dashboard

OilRadar tracks crude oil storage at Kharg Island — the terminal handling ~90% of Iran's crude exports — using Sentinel-1 radar satellite imagery. This page explains what each metric means and how to interpret it.

~6d
Data updates every ~6 days — the Sentinel-1 satellite revisit cycle over Kharg Island. Each new pass is processed automatically; you receive an email when new data is available.

How it works
SAR physics
Why a radar satellite can measure oil levels
Each tank at Kharg Island has a floating roof — a steel pontoon that rides directly on the oil surface. When the tank is full, the roof sits near the top; when empty, near the bottom.

Sentinel-1 illuminates the tanks with microwave radar pulses. The floating roof casts a shadow on the far wall of the tank. The satellite measures how strong the reflected signal is: a high roof (full tank) casts a small shadow and returns little signal; a low roof (empty tank) exposes more wall and returns a stronger signal.

This inverse relationship — lower radar return = more oil — is the physical basis of every number on this dashboard. OilRadar converts the radar backscatter (dB) from all 47 tanks into fill height, then into volume (Mb), on each satellite pass.
Metrics
Primary signal
Storage Level
The total volume of crude oil held across all 47 floating-roof tanks at Kharg Island, expressed as a percentage of the terminal's 33.2 Mb maximum capacity. This is the headline number — everything else contextualises it.
< 70% · Normal 70–85% · Elevated ≥ 85% · Critical
Rate of change
Fill Rate
How fast storage is changing — measured in million barrels per day (Mb/d) over a 7-day rolling window. A positive number means oil is accumulating; negative means tanks are draining.
Rising ↑
Iran is accumulating oil faster than it's exporting. Exports are slowing — fewer ships loading or sanctions tightening.
Falling ↓
Tanks are draining — export pace is picking up. Tankers are loading; Iran is pushing oil to market.
Directional signal
Build / Draw
A streak label that summarises the direction of storage over consecutive passes. Build means storage has been rising across recent observations. Draw means it has been falling. The number next to the label counts how many consecutive passes the streak has held.
Build
Persistent accumulation. If sustained for weeks, Iran may be struggling to find buyers or vessels — bearish signal for oil price.
Draw
Persistent export flow. Tanks clearing out suggests active loading — bullish signal consistent with export activity.
Event detection
Export Pulse
A discrete event flag triggered when storage drops by ≥ 2 Mb within a 6–12 day window. Each event corresponds to a probable VLCC tanker loading at the terminal. AIS vessel tracking typically confirms the event 7–14 days later.
High count
Multiple large loading events in a short period — elevated export activity, possible sustained drawdown cycle.
Zero / rare
No large discrete drops detected — suggests no major VLCC loadings, consistent with a build phase.
Per-tank
Tank Activity Index
A per-tank measure of how much the radar backscatter is varying over a 30-day rolling window. High variability means the tank is actively cycling (loading and refilling). Low variability means the tank is idle or at a fixed level. Visible in the Per-Tank Map section.
High activity
Tank is cycling frequently — primary loading tank. Changes here often lead aggregate storage movements by 1–2 passes.
Low / zero
Tank is static — either chronically full (strategic reserve) or offline. Not contributing to current export flow.
Anchorage · Ships
Tanker Queue Index
Weekly vessel count at Kharg anchorage derived from Sentinel-1 SAR ship detection. Each weekly pass is classified by vessel length: VLCC ≥290m, Suezmax 260–290m, Aframax 220–260m, MR/Panamax 100–220m. The Queue Index (TQI) = latest weekly count ÷ historical p95 × 100. A TQI above 85 indicates the anchorage queue is near its historical maximum — a leading indicator of export pressure and potential loading delays.
TQI > 85
High Pressure — queue near historical max. Tanker backlog building; probable loading delays or export surge imminent.
TQI < 50
Low activity — anchorage below normal. Fewer tankers waiting; consistent with sanctions pressure on buyers or low export volumes.
