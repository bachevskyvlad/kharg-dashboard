How it works

Vessel counts are derived from Sentinel-1 SAR satellite imagery captured every ~6 days. Only vessels at anchor in the waiting areas are counted — the Dardanelles transit channel itself is excluded. Data covers both the Aegean (West, Bozcaada) and Marmara Sea (East, Şarköy) anchorages from 2016 to present.

About the Dardanelles Vessel Queue

The Dardanelles Strait — also called the Çanakkale Strait — is a 61-kilometre waterway connecting the Aegean Sea to the Marmara Sea. Together with the Bosphorus it forms the Turkish Straits system, the only sea route linking the Black Sea to the Mediterranean and governed by the 1936 Montreux Convention.

Commercial tankers, bulk carriers, and container ships must wait at the West Anchorage (Bozcaada, Aegean, eastbound) or East Anchorage (Şarköy, Marmara Sea, westbound) before entering the transit channel. Current Dardanelles waiting times and queue intensity are captured by Sentinel-1 SAR satellite passes every ~6 days.

Queue Index — Methodology

The Queue Index compares the current vessel count to the 10-year monthly average for that calendar month. A value of 100 equals historical norm. Above 130 signals elevated Dardanelles traffic congestion; below 70 indicates unusually light Turkish Straits traffic.

Vessel classification by length: Capesize ≥290 m, Post-Panamax ≥260 m, Panamax ≥220 m, Handymax ≥100 m. Vessels under 100 m are excluded. Data covers both Dardanelles shipping anchorages with a 10-year record from 2016.

Why the Dardanelles Gets Congested

Dardanelles strait congestion is driven by its position as the mandatory gateway for all Black Sea traffic. Channel width narrows to just 1.2 km at Çanakkale, with strong counter-currents and restricted visibility creating transit windows. Montreux Convention rules require advance notice and restrict large tankers over 150,000 DWT to daytime passage. Surges in Black Sea crude oil and grain exports amplify queue build-up at both anchorages.

Montreux Convention & Transit Rules

Under the Montreux Convention rules for commercial ships, merchant vessels have free passage through the Turkish Straits. Ships over 150 m must provide 8 hours advance notice. VLCCs over 150,000 DWT are restricted to daytime transit only. Turkish Straits transit fees and VTS rules are enforced by Turkish maritime authorities at both the Dardanelles and Bosphorus under a single regulatory framework.
