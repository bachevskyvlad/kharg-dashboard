How it works

Vessel counts are derived from Sentinel-1 SAR satellite imagery captured every ~6 days. Only vessels at anchor in the waiting areas are counted — the narrow Dardanelles transit channel itself is excluded. Data covers the Aegean entrance (Bozcaada anchorage) and Marmara exit (Şarköy anchorage) from 2016 to present.

About the Dardanelles Vessel Queue

The Dardanelles Strait (Çanakkale Boğazı) is a 61-kilometre narrow waterway connecting the Aegean Sea to the Marmara Sea. Together with the Bosphorus it forms the Turkish Straits system — the only maritime route between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean, governed by the 1936 Montreux Convention.

Crude oil tankers from Russia, Kazakhstan, and Azerbaijan, alongside Ukrainian grain carriers, must wait at the Bozcaada anchorage (Aegean entrance) or the Şarköy anchorage (Marmara exit) before entering the strait convoy. Dardanelles waiting times and queue intensity are captured by Sentinel-1 SAR satellite passes every ~6 days.

Queue Index — Methodology

The Queue Index compares the current vessel count to the 10-year monthly average for that calendar month. A value of 100 equals historical norm. Above 130 signals elevated Dardanelles traffic congestion; below 70 indicates unusually light Turkish Straits traffic.

Vessel classification by length: Capesize ≥290 m, Post-Panamax ≥260 m, Panamax ≥220 m, Handymax ≥100 m. Vessels under 100 m are excluded. Data covers both Dardanelles anchorages with a 10-year historical record from 2016.

Why the Dardanelles Gets Congested

Dardanelles congestion is driven by three factors: the physical constraint of the 61-km channel — only 1.2 km wide at its narrowest — requiring convoy management; Montreux Convention rules restricting large tankers over 150,000 DWT to daytime-only passage; and Meltemi wind events, fog, and emergency closures that force vessels into extended anchorage waits. Black Sea crude export surges and Ukrainian grain shipments amplify queue build-ups at the Bozcaada anchorage.

Montreux Convention & Transit Rules

Under the Montreux Convention rules, commercial vessels have right of free passage through the Dardanelles. Vessels over 150 m must provide 8 hours advance notice. VLCCs over 150,000 DWT are restricted to daytime transit only. Turkish Straits VTS manages convoy scheduling and can temporarily close the strait during adverse weather or emergencies. Pilotage is not mandatory for commercial ships but recommended for vessels over 200 m.
