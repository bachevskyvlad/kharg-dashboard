Technical Methodology

How The Signal Is Built

OilRadar uses Sentinel-1 C-band SAR satellite imagery to measure physical crude oil storage volumes at floating roof tank terminals. The methodology converts radar backscatter geometry into million-barrel estimates — producing an export signal 4–7 weeks ahead of official EIA, JODI, and IEA statistics.

What Is SAR Imagery?

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) is an active microwave sensor that emits radar pulses and measures the energy reflected back to the satellite. Unlike optical satellites, SAR operates in all weather conditions and at night — cloud cover, rain, and darkness do not interrupt coverage.

OilRadar uses Sentinel-1, the European Space Agency's C-band SAR constellation (5.4 GHz, ~5.6 cm wavelength). Sentinel-1 images major oil export terminals 2–3 times per week on average, providing a dense enough time series to detect storage dynamics as they develop.

Why Floating Roof Tanks?

Above-ground crude oil storage tanks at export terminals are almost universally fitted with floating roofs — pontoon structures that rest directly on the oil surface. As oil is pumped in, the roof rises. As oil is loaded onto tankers, the roof lowers. The roof position is a direct, physical record of fill level — not a model or an estimate.

This physical relationship is what makes SAR so powerful for oil intelligence: the satellite reads the tank itself, at the source, before any tanker moves.

The Measurement Process

1

SAR Acquisition & Pre-processing

Raw Sentinel-1 IW (Interferometric Wide Swath) GRD (Ground Range Detected) scenes are downloaded for each satellite pass over the target terminal. Scenes are radiometrically calibrated to sigma-naught (σ⁰) backscatter values and orthorectified using a digital elevation model to correct for terrain distortion.

2

Floating Roof Shadow Detection

A floating roof inside a cylindrical tank creates a characteristic double-bounce radar signature: the radar signal bounces off the internal tank wall and the floating roof surface, returning a bright specular reflection to the satellite. Adjacent to this bright return is a radar shadow — a region of low backscatter where the tank wall blocks the signal from reaching the roof.

The angular geometry of the SAR acquisition and the known incidence angle allow precise calculation of the shadow height. Shadow height is proportional to the gap between the current roof position and the top of the tank wall. A tall shadow = low roof = near-empty tank. A short or absent shadow = high roof = full tank.

3

Volume Calculation

Fill height is calculated as: Tank Height − Shadow Height / sin(incidence angle). Tank diameter is known from baseline imagery, historical SAR scenes at known fill levels, or public engineering records. Volume in cubic meters equals π × (diameter/2)² × fill height, converted to million barrels (1 Mn Bbl ≈ 158,987 cubic meters).

Individual tank readings are aggregated across all measurable tanks at the terminal. Tanks obscured by neighboring structures, shipping equipment, or geometric shadow overlap are excluded. Measurement uncertainty is approximately ±0.1–0.2 Mn Bbl per terminal reading.

4

Export Signal Generation

Rolling 2–4 week storage dynamics produce the export signal:

Storage Build (Rising Volume): crude oil is accumulating at the export terminal. Tankers are queuing, loading infrastructure is constrained, or geopolitical events (sanctions, insurance disruptions, strait closures) are reducing throughput. This signal typically precedes a confirmed export bottleneck reaching official data by 4–7 weeks.

Storage Drawdown (Falling Volume): tankers are actively loading and draining the terminal. Physical crude is flowing into the global market. This supply signal appears in EIA, JODI, and IEA reports 4–8 weeks later, after tankers deliver cargoes and agencies compile survey data.

5

Geopolitical Event Overlay

Export signal readings are correlated with a curated geopolitical event database: sanctions announcements, insurance premium changes, shipping route disruptions, and political developments. This layer quantifies how specific event types translate into physical storage dynamics and the typical lag before market impact materialises.
